# Visual review

Compared the original Bella frames with desktop, tablet and phone screenshots, then decoded the recorded scroll into contact sheets. The final showcase recordings use actual wheel input, without the automated test's intermediate screenshot crops.

The first read is now a large white Max, with the nozzle plume and physical cabinet depth visible. The orange panel, blue sky, compact navigation, cream product row and editorial split carry the reference's hierarchy. The floor composition puts the 46.7 cm unit beside the sofa at approximately seat height. It no longer reads as a miniature desktop accessory.

The three material problems found during visual iteration were: the cabinet crossing the room caption; the desktop menu covering the hero; and the phone canvas extending beyond the page near landing. These were fixed with caption clearance timing, an expanding header, and a crop-aware floor anchor. The subsequent recording shows a continuous descent and a stable floor position. Contact shadows remain deliberately soft on the rug. The hero remains larger than Bella's appliance because the featured product is a broad floor cabinet.

The secondary 3D scent bottles now have actual shoulder, neck and rib geometry; light reflections change with rotation. All 18 use the same original 120 ml packaging shape, as shown in the catalog. They do not invent scent-specific label designs. The dropdown preserves five clean icon links, keyboard focus, arrow navigation and Escape.

Self-assessment (not an external award or user approval): design 8, usability 8, creativity 7, content 8; weighted 7.8/10. The limit is the composite stock setting versus Bella's bespoke, unified 3D environment, plus the licensed font substitution. These are not represented as exact visual parity.

The taste inspector reports no broken images, horizontal overflow or covered headlines. Its source lint still flags legacy eyebrow labels, table/form rules, real floor shadows and photographic scrims across the wider site. Its runtime flags include a false association between a review author's name and the later decorative “bonjour”, reference-derived small typography, and too few DOM entrances/no video. Adding popping section entrances or swapping this continuous 3D scene for footage would contradict the user's direction. The palette and mobile banner line-wrap findings were corrected after this report. The original unfiltered inspection output is retained; no claim is made that all subjective design rules pass.
